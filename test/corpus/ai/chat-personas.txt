Personas can be a powerful tool for keeping your team focused on real user needs, but they also come with some potential pitfalls. Here's a balanced overview to help you decide.

**Pros**

- **Shared understanding:** Personas give your whole team a common language for talking about users.
- **Empathy:** Putting a name and a face to research findings helps stakeholders connect with the people they're designing for.
- **Prioritization:** When you're weighing features, personas can help you ask, "Which of these would actually matter to Maria?"

**Cons**

- **Risk of stereotypes:** If personas aren't grounded in real research, they can reinforce assumptions rather than challenge them.
- **They can go stale:** User needs evolve over time, and a persona created three years ago may no longer reflect reality.
- **Overemphasis on demographics:** Details like age or hobbies can distract from what really matters, which is goals, behaviors, and pain points.

**My recommendation**

If you decide to use personas, base them on actual interviews and data, focus on behaviors rather than demographics, and revisit them regularly. Many teams also find success with lighter-weight alternatives like jobs-to-be-done statements.
