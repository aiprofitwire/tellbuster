A heuristic review is when a few people look through a product and check it against a list of known usability principles. A common list says, for example, that a system should show people what is happening, use words they know, and help them recover from mistakes.

It is fast and cheap. Two or three reviewers can go through the main tasks in a day and list the problems they see, with a note on how serious each one is.

It is not a replacement for testing with users. Reviewers are experts, not your users. They will find obvious problems, like a missing back button or an unclear label. They will miss problems that come from how real people think about the task.

Use it early to clean up the easy problems before you test. That way, your user sessions can focus on the harder questions.

Have each reviewer work alone first, then combine the lists. People working alone find more problems than a group looking at the same screen together.
