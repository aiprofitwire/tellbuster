Remote workshops don't have to be painful.

I've facilitated over 100 virtual workshops in the past two years. Here's what actually works:

1. Keep it short. 90 minutes max. Attention spans on video are real.

2. Send the agenda early. People engage more when they know what's coming.

3. Use a shared board. Tools like Miro or Mural give everyone a place to contribute, not just the loudest voices.

4. Build in silent work. Five minutes of individual brainstorming before discussion leads to better, more diverse ideas.

5. Take real breaks. Not "let's keep going, we're almost done." Real breaks.

6. End with clear next steps. Who's doing what, by when.

The biggest shift for me? Realizing that a remote workshop isn't an in-person workshop on a screen. It's a completely different format with its own strengths.

When you design for remote from the start, you can actually include more voices than ever before.
