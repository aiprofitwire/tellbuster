Great question! Understanding the difference between active and passive voice can significantly improve the clarity of your writing.

**Active voice**

In the active voice, the subject of the sentence performs the action.

- *The team reviewed the proposal.*
- *Maria submitted the form.*

**Passive voice**

In the passive voice, the subject receives the action. The person or thing doing the action is often moved to the end of the sentence or left out entirely.

- *The proposal was reviewed by the team.*
- *The form was submitted.*

**Why it matters**

Active voice is generally clearer, more direct, and easier to read. It makes it obvious who is responsible for what, which is particularly important in instructions, policies, and legal documents.

**When passive voice is okay**

That said, passive voice isn't always wrong. It can be useful when:

- The person performing the action is unknown or unimportant ("The building was constructed in 1920").
- You want to emphasize the result rather than the actor.

**Quick tip:** If you can add "by zombies" after the verb and the sentence still makes sense, it's probably passive! For example, "The form was submitted (by zombies)."
