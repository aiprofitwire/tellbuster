# Source: written by an AI model for this test (style: plain, asked to avoid filler). Prompt: "Explain how to take useful meeting notes. Write plainly. Short sentences, no filler, no headings." License: MIT, like this repo.

Useful notes answer three questions: what did we decide, who is doing what, and by when. Everything else is optional.

Put decisions and action items at the top. People who missed the meeting will read the first few lines and stop.

Write each action item with a name and a date. "Look into vendor options" will not happen. "Dana compares three vendors and shares a list by May 3" might.

You don't need a transcript. If a long discussion ended in a decision, write the decision and one line on why. If it ended without one, write that too, along with the open question.

Share the notes the same day, while people still remember the meeting. Ask them to reply with corrections within a day.

Keep all notes for a project in one place, in order, with the date in the title. Months later, someone will need to know when and why a choice was made, and a searchable list of notes will answer that in a minute.
