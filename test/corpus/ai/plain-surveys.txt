# Source: written by an AI model for this test (style: plain, asked to avoid filler). Prompt: "Explain how to write a good survey. Write plainly. Short sentences, no filler, no headings." License: MIT, like this repo.

Decide what you will do with the answers before you write a single question. If an answer won't change a decision, drop the question.

Keep it short. Ten questions is plenty for most surveys. Every extra question means fewer people finish.

Ask one thing at a time. "How satisfied are you with our prices and service?" is two questions. Split it.

Avoid leading words. "How much did you enjoy the event?" assumes they enjoyed it. "How was the event?" does not.

Give balanced answer choices, with as many bad options as good ones, and a way to say "I don't know" or "Doesn't apply."

Put easy questions first and personal ones, like age or income, at the end. Make those optional.

Add one open question, like "What is one thing we should change?" It often gives you the most useful answers.

Try the survey on three or four people before you send it. Watch where they hesitate or ask what you mean. Rewrite those questions, then send it out.
