Accessibility is not a feature. It's a foundation.

Let that sink in.

Over 1 billion people worldwide live with some form of disability. That's roughly 1 in 6 people.

Yet so many products still treat accessibility as an afterthought, something to "fix later" if there's time.

Here's the thing: when you design for accessibility, everyone benefits.

📱 Captions help people watching videos on a noisy train.
🔠 Clear contrast helps people reading in bright sunlight.
⌨️ Keyboard navigation helps power users move faster.
🧠 Plain language helps people who are tired, stressed, or distracted.

This is called the curb-cut effect. Designs that remove barriers for some people end up making life easier for all of us.

Accessibility isn't just the right thing to do. It's good business, good design, and in many cases, the law.

If your team hasn't made accessibility a priority yet, today is a great day to start.
